# Bounties

**Bounties** are a task-based funding mechanism where a funder defines a task, sets a reward, and contributors claim the bounty by completing the work. It's a straightforward "you do this, you get that" model emphasizing speed, clarity, and minimal friction.

## How It Works

Bounties create a direct link between defined work and compensation.

1. **Define the task** — A funder specifies clear deliverables, scope, acceptance criteria, and reward amount.
2. **Post the bounty** — The bounty is published on forums or bounty platforms (such as Dework or Gitcoin), or through smart contracts.
3. **Contributors claim and execute** — One or more contributors pick up the task and deliver results.
4. **Review and accept** — A reviewer evaluates the submission against the acceptance criteria.
5. **Payment releases** — Upon acceptance, the reward is released. Smart contract escrow can optionally support trustless payments.

## Advantages

- Reduces friction between funders and contributors.
- Creates execution-focused incentives with clear expectations.
- Attracts talent through accessible, low-commitment entry points.
- Distributes work efficiently across decentralized networks.

## Limitations

- Can struggle with complex or collaborative projects that require sustained coordination.
- Is not ideal for building long-term working relationships.
- Handles ambiguous or evolving requirements poorly.
- Requires a clearly defined scope and verifiable deliverables.

## Best Used When

- Deliverables are measurable in design, development, research, or content.
- Funding needs to be distributed quickly with minimal governance overhead.
- New contributors need an accessible way to participate.
- Tasks are execution-heavy, well-scoped, and have verifiable outcomes.

## Examples and Use Cases

### Bug Fixes and Documentation

Public goods networks use bounties for bug fixes, documentation improvements, and UX research — work that is well-scoped and independently completable.

### Translation and Localization

Community labs fund multilingual resource translations through bounties, allowing contributors worldwide to participate.

### Design and Graphics

DAOs issue bounties for protocol explanatory graphics, social media assets, and UI/UX improvements.
